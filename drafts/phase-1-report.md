## 1. Summary

**Summary:** Retail staff at ten partner companies discussed the Grower Portal. Conversations focused on the impact the portal has on doing business with growers, with a goal to understand what matters most when connecting growers and retailers. A grower survey with 2,015 respondents is used to contrast retailer and grower perspectives.[^survey]

- Efficiency begins when growers can access the information co-op sees and complete workflows without making a phone call. Impactful workflows include grain bids, contracts, offers, and scale tickets, agronomy booking and visibility into completed jobs.
- Efficiency stops when growers cannot find, sign, or pay in the portal as expected. This leads to time consuming troubleshooting and phone calls. 
- When asked about their vision for where the portal can be more impactful, most partners had unique ideas. Yet the overall theme was clear: More of the Grower business integrated with the portal. 



## 2. Methodology

From July 14 through August 28, 2026, we talked with staff at ten partner companies. The partners on those calls sell to growers, support portal administration, or own customer relationships, and someone from the AgVend product team ran each conversation with a standardized set of questions. We wanted to hear how the Grower Portal is used, where it falls short, and where it should evolve. Partner conversations are one piece of the overarching plan. The next piece will include conversations with the Grower Portal users themselves. 

**How we selected partners.** The ten were chosen using a Partner Matrix that accounted for several factors with the aim to represent a diversity of partners. Factors include grower usage from partners with 31,000 users (Keystone) down to 396 users (South West Terminal), and the list also spread annual revenue, the ERP behind the portal, service tier, and geography. CenDak was an intentional pick: it ranked 41st by unique users and 16th by product events, which is high use for its size.[^selection]

**How we selected individuals.** Three persona groups were classified to represent the roles that are most involved with the portal: someone who owns the grower relationship, someone who sells, and someone who runs the portal day to day. Next we identified who fits these roles at the partner companies we selected, and although the 19 people we talked to had unique titles, the persona groups held true to their role in Grower Portal.

:::chart-sheet

**What we asked.** Within a persona group, people heard the same open questions, with minor tweaks based on their business unit (i.e. grain and agronomy). The questions aimed to understand how the portal worked well, where it didn't, and where it should go in the future. No one was shown a mockup or asked to react to a concept. Every call ended on the same three questions: what we failed to ask, what change over the next year would show we listened, and whether they could introduce a few growers, including someone who barely uses the portal.

**Grower survey data as a baseline.** An older in-portal survey sits beside these calls as a check. It holds about 2,015 responses from September 2022 through March 2025, across 25 or more partners, prompted after a grower had already logged in. It covers more companies than these ten, and it was not how the ten were chosen. Where a finding below cites a count, the count comes from this survey.

## 3. What partners already rely on

Partner staff rely on the Grower Portal because a grower can see the same workflows the co-op sees and take the next step without making phone calls. Grain staff describe those workflows as bids, contracts, offers, and scale tickets, and agronomy staff refer to bookings and completed jobs. The portal's impact is improving visibility and efficiency on both sides of the retailer/grower relationship. Their communications are less about information relay, and more about decision making. 

**Grain Contracts.** An operations lead at South West Terminal compared workflows before and after the Grower Portal. Grain contracts go out through the portal and come back signed, though some of her growers are still on fax machines and she waits on those. The portal keeps the copy and returns it faster than a fax sent and a fax waited on. Before this, the co-op's grower site was Grow Central, and it lived on a computer. Staff also took calls from people who needed a statement and had to wait until someone was back in the office at 8. She now points them at the portal, and if they are in the truck, the record is already there.[^swt-ops]

**Grower self-service.** A Keystone grain merchandiser sees the same shift on grain. Growers used to call and ask how many bushels were sold, or keep it in pen and paper. One grower who does not use a smartphone has the contracts bookmarked, and his partner opens them every day. From the tractor he can see what is already sold and sells more without guessing.

**Agronomy bookings.** A Keystone agronomy seller sees it on its side of the business too. A booking on paper could get lost. Growers now see the order and get a notification when the spreading job is done, including when a field was missed, so the "where are you?" calls dropped. On a sales call he can send the quote while they are still talking, and both of them are looking at the same plan.

The efficiency is the work that leaves the phone. Staff stop retrieving statements, bushel counts, and job status, and growers stop waiting on the mail, a fax, or the office to open. What remains is the conversation that needs a person: pricing grain, confirming a quote, catching a missed field job. Staff said the leftover time goes back into the rest of the day. Growers tell each other to sign up, and some praise the portal without being asked, reflects back positively on the retailer's business. 

**Survey data supports positive claims.** Growers have said this in volume, too. In an in-portal survey of 2,015 responses from 2022 through 2025, pricing and contracts came up about 135 times, and about 150 responses were simply positive, which matches what the staff is seeing. 

### Modal

- A River Valley grain originator. Offers used to wait on a phone call during business hours, and paperwork could take about 10 days in the mail. Growers now place offers at night when the market moves and sign contracts on the portal. He pitches grain, agronomy, energy, and feed in one place, though daily use, from his seat, is still grain.
- An agronomy lead at CenDak. Bills used to sit in the mail for weeks while growers were in the field, and they now see the load when it happens. He and the grower look at the same invoices, which is how errors get caught while they are still on the phone.



## 4. Where the same work breaks down

Partners described real efficiency from having contracts, offers, bookings, and invoices in one place, and that efficiency stops when growers cannot find, sign, or pay in the portal as expected. In rare cases, partners lack visibility into grower activities, which leads to phone calls made by both parties to resolve issues.

**Grain offers can lack visibility**. Months after placing an offer, a Keystone grower calls the grain merchandiser: I feel like I made an offer back in January. Is it still out there? Staff can see the offer, but the grower has no tab for what is still open. On a fast market day the co-op may be entering dozens, and if one is missed, nothing on the grower's screen proves it was there. He wants that list signed and visible the way a contract already is, so the call is "I see the offer," not "did you lose it?"[^keystone-grain]

**Payment issues.** At NEW Coop the call is about money that will not attach to the bill. An energy lead's customers can open an invoice and download it, and they still cannot pay it from that screen. She sends them to the statement, tells them to pay a portion, and has them write which invoice the money is for. A fuel bill split with a landlord or a tenant is the case that makes the extra step matter: the invoice is already on the screen, the payment happens one step away, and someone in accounting has to apply it.[^newcoop-energy]

**Finding the correct customer to notify.** CenDak's bookkeepers do not always get that call. An agronomy lead there says growers do act on invoices, bookings, and new contracts, and the notice that they did so is not consistent. The team has tried to route those emails to the right people, and some of the gap may be how each customer is set up. What the bookkeepers still ask for is a reliable signal, because the grower may have finished the step and the co-op is not sure.

The cost becomes the time spent on phone calls the portal was designed to eliminate. Staff explain where to click, which filter is hiding the contract, or which invoice a payment belongs to. An offer can sit unseen until someone remembers it, and a payment can arrive without a reliable signal that it arrived.

Growers wrote about the payment version of this more than any other topic. In the same survey of 2,015 responses, invoices, billing, and payments came up about 220 times, and confusing navigation came up about 45. The interviews show the missed click on a contract, an offer, or an invoice, while growers, unprompted, mostly wrote about paying.[^survey]

### Modal

- A River Valley grain originator. Growers call because they cannot find a contract that needs a signature. Sometimes the sync is slow. Sometimes they have one entity selected and the offer or ticket is under another. He walks them through the clicks. The offer form adds a second miss: quantity has no comma, so 5,000 and 50,000 look alike, and the price field opens on a zero they have to delete.
- A marketing lead at Heritage. Invoices, bookings, purchase history, quotes, and statements look the same at a glance, especially on a phone. Someone in the portal every day learns the tile, and someone who is not has to work to pick the right one.
- NEW Coop's chief executive. Patronage equity is the grower's money in the co-op, and he says people dig through the account, then the type, then the class, to find it. The balance is in the portal, and the path is not obvious.



## 5. Partner's vision for the portal

The last questions on each call asked for a bigger step than the work already on the portal: what would you change, what would you pay more for, and what are peers doing that you wish you were doing? The jobs they named do not match. An agronomist, a CEO, and a sales director each described something the portal does not do today, and where a topic came up more than once, the job inside it still changed with the seat.

**Robust farm mapping.** A Keystone agronomy seller's pick, when he had to choose one, was the map. On that side of the business a field opens as its own small picture, and the farm next to it is a separate screen, with no roads between them. An applicator has driven through a yard to reach a field. He wants to zoom out, see the two farms together, and drop a pin for the driveway before the truck leaves. That is a field tool, separate from the contract, the offer, or the invoice.

**Chat based intelligence.** NEW Coop's chief executive answered the pay-more question with a question he wants the grower to ask the app: "How many bushels came in last fall?" or "How many gallons of Roundup did I buy?" He does not want them to walk the screens to find it. He also wants the co-op's vehicle tracking on the grower side, so a farmer can see the truck that is coming to the farm. He has raised the tracking idea more than once, and nobody else in these calls put either of those at the top.

**CRM Automation.** A Keystone energy sales director was not talking about the grower. His team types the week's visits into an email because notes in the system do not become the report, and he wants the notes entered once so a summary can go to the manager. An agronomy lead at CenDak and an executive at Valley Wide also talked about CRM, and they did not ask for that report. The CenDak lead wants a customer's history to follow them when the salesperson changes. The Valley Wide executive would rather that work live in the portal than in Salesforce, and he said the co-op is not far enough along to say what they would pay more for.

The other wishes scatter the same way. One grain originator wants percent of the crop sold on the portal, one merchandiser has heard a few farmers ask for a chart on the cash bid, and one marketer, pressed for a wish, asked for help resizing a banner. Each of those is real, and each of them is one seat.

**One unified platform for everything on the operation.** A Keystone energy sales director, asked what growers want next, said one place, where grain, agronomy, or energy pricing and markets should live. NEW Coop's chief executive wants the co-op to be the stop for products, services, and advice, and to make it painless to do business there. A River Valley grain originator already points growers at grain, agronomy, energy, and feed in one portal, and the step he wants next is percent of the crop sold, in that same place. A map, a truck on the way to the farm, a load number, or a price chart still lives in a second app, an email, or a phone call, and the ask is to bring it here.

The survey has the same shape. Most of the 2,015 responses pile up on paying, price, a phone app, and getting in, and under that a handful of ideas show up from the people who do that work. Fuel and propane came up about 25 times, mostly once energy partners were in the survey: tank levels, ordering fuel, an invoice that lags the fill. Field maps and soil data came up about 15 times, concentrated at one partner. Grain growers at River Valley asked for things almost no one else wrote down, such as several delivery locations on one screen, cattle futures, and basis in color. The survey does not mention a weekly sales report or a grower asking how many gallons they bought, but a small number, from the seat that uses it, asked for the next piece of the account to be in the portal.

These calls cannot rank which job comes first, though they can say where partners want that work to go. The portal is the place a grower does business with the co-op, and the seats want the next piece of that business on it. Growers who wrote in were not picking from that list, though a few of them were already asking for the same kind of move.

### Modal

- The Keystone agronomy seller also described load numbers that staff email or text to drivers who pick up product. One grower had asked him for the same thing. When he had to choose the change that would mean the team had listened, he chose the map.

His answer for this year, the one that would show the team had listened, was the offer form: a comma in the quantity, and no default zero in the price. That repair is in section 4.

- A CenDak marketer. Asked for any marketing tool she wanted, she said a creative assistant that would resize a banner, which is a staff job rather than a grower one.
- A Richardson Pioneer executive wants the inbox to carry a network-wide offer and a local one, and he wants Goose and supplier onboarding to keep improving. He said the inbox work is already underway.
- The Valley Wide executive would pay for a retail module along the lines of energy. He also said the co-op is still early in using the portal, so this is the wrong place to price the next version.
- At Frontier, the wish was sending growers the as-applied and soil maps that now go out as emailed PDFs. That is a different map from the roads and driveways above.



## 6. (WIP) What these calls cannot answer

Staff described what growers can already see, what they call about, and what they have stopped calling about, which is enough to say what the co-op relies on and where a contract, an offer, a booking, or an invoice breaks. It is not enough to say what the grower would ask for if the co-op were not in the room.

Answers to known issues still need attention. A grower who placed an offer in January may need that offer on the screen, or may only notice it is missing when they call. A grower splitting a fuel bill with a landlord or a tenant may accept paying from the statement, or may be calling because that step should not exist. A farm that shares one login may want one person to see the price and another to pay. Staff at more than one co-op described that split, and no grower has.

The survey does not answer how issues should be solved. The 2,015 responses came from people who were already in the portal, across more partners than these ten and across 2022 through 2025, and they wrote most often about invoices, billing, and payments. They were not asked about an open offer or a shared login. A few asked for field maps, and they were not asked to rank a truck on the way to the farm, a question to the phone, or a weekly sales report.

The return visit was part of the ask. We said we would come back and talk to a few growers, with the staff on these calls sitting in, including someone who uses the portal and someone who barely does. The point is to check what staff described against the person who opens it. Until those conversations happen, a one-seat idea stays a one-seat idea.
