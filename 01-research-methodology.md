# Research Methodology — Phase 1 (Partner Interviews)

*Source: #grower-portal-research Slack channel, Jun–Sep 2026*

## Goal

Understand how AgVend's Customer (Grower) Portal is actually being used and where it falls short, by talking directly to partner-side staff (agronomy, grain, accounting, admin) who interact with the portal or support growers who use it. This phase was explicitly a precursor to Phase 2: talking to **growers themselves**.

## Partner selection

Ten partners were selected to represent the full range of how the product is used across the customer base, based on:

- **Usage volume** — from Keystone Cooperative (~31K users) to South West Terminal (396 users)
- **ARR** — from ~$104K to ~$1.4M
- **ERP diversity** — seven different ERPs represented
- **Service tier** — three different tiers
- **Geography** — Midwest, Southeast, Mountain West, and Canada

One deliberate outlier pick was **CenDak**, ranked 41st by unique users but 16th by product events (i.e., high engagement relative to size) — included to test whether engagement intensity surfaces different feedback than raw usage volume.

Before locking the list, the team looped in Partner Success (Megan Schmit) and Sales (Ryan Risdal) to flag any partner relationships where research conversations could be awkward, counterproductive, or — conversely — valuable for deepening the relationship or supporting an active deal.

**Target: 10 partners, ideally 3 distinct 1:1 interviews each** (three personas per partner — see below).

## Personas targeted per partner

The research proposal defined three interview personas per partner. The original Google Doc is not readable from this project (see the link in `00-project-overview.md`). The script below was reconstructed from the transcripts, where interviewers say they are reading a shared list so every person in a persona hears the same questions. Daniel Randolph calls it out as seven preset questions on the Dan Dix call (2026-07-20). John Vink reads the same sets on the first South West Terminal calls (2026-07-14 and 2026-07-15), and the calls end with the same last questions regardless of persona (the ones John describes as highlighted on the sheet).

Interviewers mixed questions across personas when the seat did not match the script (Stephanie Leapaldt's marketing role pulled the operations list toward campaigns and measurement; grain merchandisers had agronomy wording swapped). They also asked spontaneous follow-ups. Those probes are not part of the standardized set. Later additions that show up once, not as repeating script lines, include multi-user / shared accounts, who the decision-maker is on the farm, and comfort with AI features.

### Last questions (asked at the end, regardless of persona)

1. Is there anything you expected us to ask, or wanted to get on the table, that we didn't?
2. If we changed the portal over the next year, what would make you feel we listened to this conversation?
3. Can you introduce us to a few growers (active or disengaged) for the next round? Partners can sit in.

### Executive sponsor

1. When you think about your relationship with growers, what matters most? What does a strong grower relationship look like for the business?
2. Where does the grower portal fit in that picture today, and is it playing the role you expected when you started with AgVend?
3. Looking at other retailers, peers, or competitors, is there anything they are doing with customer tools that you wish you were doing?
4. If the portal worked exactly how you wanted, what would it be doing for the business, and what would that be worth?
5. Has the work your team has put into adoption been worth it?
6. Is there a version of the portal that would be worth paying more for on its own, and what would that version need to do? (Framed as a priority exercise, not a pricing pitch.)
7. Is there anything you would tell us to stop doing or change?

### Sales rep

Grain vs. agronomy wording was swapped depending on the seat.

1. Walk us through how you interact with growers from the start of the season to the end. Where, if at all, does the portal come up?
2. If a grower is not using it, do you recommend it, and how do you pitch it?
3. Have you seen growers use it in a way that surprised you, positively or negatively?
4. Do you get complaints, or things you have to help them with often?
5. Where in the season, or between agronomy and grain, is the portal most useful, and where is it least useful?
6. If you could change one thing about how your growers interact with the portal, what would it be?
7. Are there tools missing, farmer-facing or for you, that would make the job easier or let growers self-serve?

### Operations / admin / digital lead

1. Introduce your role, and what managing the portal looks like day to day. What takes most of your time?
2. How do you interact with growers who use it? Do questions come through sales or from growers directly?
3. What is the hardest part of onboarding a new grower, and where does that process break down?
4. How often do you get support requests or questions about the portal, and what are they usually about?
5. What are you doing manually today that should just work automatically?
6. How does the portal connect to the rest of the stack (ERP, CRM, marketing tools), and where are the gaps?
7. Are growers using it differently than you expected?
8. When something goes wrong, what does escalation look like, and how long does it take to resolve?
9. What is the biggest operational headache on the customer side that we have not already covered?

## Method notes and lessons learned (from team retro discussion, Jul 20 2026)

- The team used an **open-questions approach** ("what would you change?") rather than testing specific hypotheses. For a newer, largely satisfied partner (South West Terminal), this produced limited signal — the team noted they "weren't probing enough" and speculated that testing concrete concepts (e.g., "how would you feel about a grower profitability calculator?") might surface more useful feedback.
- This is a directly relevant lesson for **Phase 2 (grower interviews)**: consider pairing open discovery questions with a few concrete concept probes.
- Interview recordings were captured via Granola and Fireflies and shared in-channel; some calls were missed by the Granola MCP integration and had to be manually shared.
- Partners that request it (e.g., Keystone) get a recap/summary of the conversations their team members participated in — commitment made to share overall learnings with all study participant companies once complete.
